# Choptank Electric Cooperative, Maryland

The local catalog identifies residential Schedule R, commercial Schedule C and Small Power Schedule S. All three are blocked for the January 2025–December 2026 request. Each requires a Maryland environmental surcharge that Choptank’s tariff incorporates by reference. The PSC’s annual index identifies the notices. Its three linked documents returned HTTP 403, and no independent dated Choptank rate sheet or public-sector invoice recovered in this review established the numerical factors. The simulator offers zero executable cycles. [Source ledger](utility_sources/choptank_md/sources.json) · [Maryland PSC general notices](https://webpscxb.pscmaryland.com/dms/general-notices)

The March 2025 and July 2026 books retain the December 1, 2023 base charges. In both books, physical pages 55–59 give R/C and pages 64–66 give S. Each schedule has the consumer charge as its minimum monthly delivery charge. The residential/commercial threshold is in kVA. A simulated load in kW cannot establish an account’s capacity or rate assignment. Seasonal RS/CS/SS add 25% under the tariff. The ordinary accounts here exclude seasonal, irrigation, coincident-energy and competitive-supply arrangements. [March 2025 book](https://choptankelectric.coop/sites/default/files/03.01.2025%20Choptank%20Electric%20Tariff%20and%20Terms%20and%20Conditions.pdf) · [July 2026 book](https://choptankelectric.coop/sites/default/files/Tariff%20updates%207-1-26.pdf)

Choptank calls S “Small Power.” Three-phase accounts can qualify with demand below 50 kW. The single-phase provision addresses service over 50 kVA where required/requested distribution capacity exceeds 50 kVA or billing demand exceeds 50 kW. Assignment lasts at least twelve billing months. After that, transfer out requires each of the previous twelve demands below 50 kW and capacity below 50 kVA. Billing demand reaching 100 kW triggers transfer to M. The distribution maximum is the highest consecutive 15-minute demand in the month. Metered reactive energy becomes billable when the measured average power factor is below 90%. A complete S bill also needs independently established SOS generation and transmission demand determinants. [Tariff §903](https://choptankelectric.coop/sites/default/files/Tariff%20updates%207-1-26.pdf) · [Cooperative Small Power bill explainer](https://choptankelectric.coop/sites/default/files/Bill%20explainer_small%20power_11.27.19.pdf)

## Non-export and PJM

Tariff §114 covers approved parallel operation of generators and storage. Section 920 identifies on-site consumption offsets separately from net metering and energy sales, requires final operating approval, and lists a no-charge Level 1 inverter application for equipment at or below 20 kW. The cooperative FAQ distinguishes storage physically prevented from injecting power from a device able to inject. These provisions establish an approval path for the exact installation. They do not establish every account’s settlement or eligibility. All equipment modes remain blocked with the tariff. [Tariff §§114 and 920](https://choptankelectric.coop/sites/default/files/Tariff%20updates%207-1-26.pdf) · [Storage and interconnection FAQ](https://choptankelectric.coop/solar)

Choptank distributes power and supplies retail SOS. Old Dominion Electric Cooperative is its wholesale supplier. The Choptank supplier tariff §8.6 describes hourly system metering by Delmarva Power & Light. Section 9 covers PJM reconciliation for suppliers. No site-specific pricing node was verified. Regional PJM data, wholesale losses and LMPs carry no retail rate or settlement entitlement.
