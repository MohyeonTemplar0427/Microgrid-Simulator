# Duke Energy Ohio electric RS and DM

The regional adapter uses Duke Energy Ohio's P.U.C.O. Electric No. 19 Standard Service Offer for Ohio electric accounts. It supports complete calendar cycles for **June 2025 and October 2026**, with a matching final monthly meter-read date, revenue month and full monthly charge. Other periods, partial and shifted cycles, estimated reads, third-party suppliers, aggregation, and alternate schedules reject. Duke Energy Ohio is the distribution utility and SSO supplier. Ohio is the retail jurisdiction. PJM **DEOK** is grid metadata, with any wholesale pricing node and PJM regional-average carbon handled separately.

## Account eligibility

- **RS, Sheet 30.18** covers ordinary, solely domestic service in the study's single-meter, single-phase 120/240 V secondary scope.
- **DM, Sheet 43.20** covers ordinary small-secondary accounts Duke assigns based on average monthly demand of at most 15 kW. This implementation requires a retained assignment after the initial term, the 12 actual consecutive demands from Duke's most recent annual review, the history end month, and confirmation that DM applies for the entire cycle without a pending DS reassignment. If that average exceeds 15 kW or at least two months exceed 30 kW, Duke may transfer the account to DS and the study rejects. A peak equal to 30 kW does not exceed 30 kW. Simulated imports do not classify an account. DM has an energy-block bill and no billed demand charge. The special nonprofit, fair, and agricultural exceptions, initial-term classification, three-phase service, DS and multiple meters are outside this scope.
- Require an ordinary non-PIPP account, independent UE-GEN applicability, and no additional local charge, self-assessment, optional rider, or special facilities. For grid-only counterfactuals the existing tariff, supplier, DM history and UE-GEN applicability carry over.
- PV and paired storage require independent confirmation that Duke approved the exact non-export installation, ordinary tariff continuation without special charges, and active metering before the cycle. The study limits native load and imports to 25 kW RS or 15 kW DM, and combined PV/storage capacity to 25 kW. It models PV-only battery charging, zero export and no credit balance. These limits define the study scope. They do not establish utility permission or equipment eligibility.

## Filed arithmetic

The $8 RS or $12 single-phase DM distribution customer charge is the filed minimum. RS distribution energy is $0.039693/kWh. DM charges $0.048863/kWh for the first 2,800 kWh and $0.004339/kWh thereafter. The filing prints a separate 3,200 kWh middle block at the same latter rate. The complete modeled bill stays above the base minimum at zero and throughout the ordinary usage envelope, so no extra minimum adjustment is added.

Sheet 25.8 generally applies revisions by the final monthly meter read. Both supported periods are bounded to matching calendar cycles, with the effective June 2 ESRR revision applicable to the June 30 final read. RC and RE follow the revenue/billing season. RS has a single supply block for June 2025 and an October 2026 winter break at 1,000 kWh. DM RC changes after 6,000 kWh and RE at 2,800 and 6,000 kWh. BTR and its RTEP credit are separate from retail capacity, energy, AER-R and SCR. Base percentage riders use distribution customer plus distribution energy only. The October index points to DCI Sheet 103.46, which sets 18.624%. The separate utility copy supplies that revision because the compiled book contains superseded Sheet 103.45. Sheet-specific applicability governs later changes to DDR, DSMR, PIPP and PSR. The full dated sheet and PDF page map is in the [source ledger](utility_sources/duke_ohio/sources.json).

Rider OET Sheet 83.6 uses fixed retail-bill blocks at 2,000 and 15,000 delivered kWh, at $0.00465, $0.00419 and $0.00363/kWh. Duke's calculator charges corroborate those blocks. The non-30-day distributor remittance rules in R.C. 5727.81 are distinct from the filed retail rider modeled here. R.C. 5739.02(B)(7) exempts electricity delivered through wires from sales tax. The model applies no CAT gross-up. June uses USR and October uses its PIPP replacement for eligible retail usage, even though the study account is non-PIPP. SGF and LGR are zero in October following the August 14, 2025 revisions.

Raw filed-rate arithmetic drives dispatch and numerical rebilling. The engine retains raw and component-rounded study values. Duke's spreadsheet rounds particular distribution, RC and RE blocks, so its displayed totals can differ from raw arithmetic. The engine does not reproduce invoice rounding or optimize rounded cash charges. See the [validation report](Duke_Energy_Ohio_Implementation_Validation.md) for the observed deltas and evidence limits.
