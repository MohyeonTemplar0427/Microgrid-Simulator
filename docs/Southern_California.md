# Southern California billing candidate

Tariff data version: `socal-2026-09-29.1`. This is a bounded recurring-bill and dispatch implementation, not complete reproduction of every account bill. See the handoff for the tested engine pin and browser cases.

## Territory and account identity

Delivery uses the existing California Energy Commission distribution polygons, AgencyNum 58970 (LADWP) and 86250 (SCE). City/county text never selects a provider. Point intersections plus a 100 m boundary screen return approximate, ambiguous or unsupported; only recorded bill/utility attestation produces verified status. Overlapping Metropolitan Water District polygons are preserved, not silently discarded. CCA polygons supply availability suggestions only.

The existing service step includes a bill-confirmed delivery override; original map evidence, retrieval/version metadata, response hashes and confirmation reference remain saved. Avoid account numbers in references. Delivery does not establish generation enrollment. SCE bundled and CCA selections are stored separately. Actual unsupported CCA bills are rejected by the API as well as the form; a separately labelled hypothetical bundled comparison is allowed.

Climate regions are **account-confirmed inputs**, not inferred from the delivery polygon. LADWP zones 1/2 and SCE baseline regions 5/6/8/9/10/13/14/15/16 are supported. Automatic address-to-climate-region mapping is not implemented. The confirmation and selected region are saved with the site. Basic/all-electric allocation and heat-pump water-heater allowance require account confirmation.

## Billing and dispatch

- LADWP R-1A uses zone-dependent tier blocks, independently supplied annual PAC tier, capped-ordinance minimum and incremental charges, with consumption allocated by billing days across seasonal/quarterly boundaries. PAC is not inferred from the study month's consumption. R-1A rejects annual monthly average at or above 3,000 kWh.
- LADWP TOU uses weekday high peak 13:00–17:00, low peak 10:00–13:00 and 17:00–20:00, otherwise base. No SCE holiday rule is reused. Commercial facilities ratchets require all 11 previous monthly peaks, including the 4/30 kW minimum. A-2/A-3 reactive-charge cases are rejected unless confirmed outside that charge's scope.
- SCE keeps delivery and bundled generation separate. FRC is added once; CCA-only MCAM is not added to bundled service. D tiers and TOU baseline credits use daily seasonal quantities from Preliminary Statement H. PRIME requires qualifying technology.
- SCE TOU honors the filed holidays (including Monday observation when specified holidays fall on Sunday, not Friday observation for Saturday). Demand uses 15-minute maxima and nearest-whole-kW billing. The optimizer uses integer peak variables to match that rounding.
- Fixed monthly quantities use the explicit billing-month factor. A days/30 default in the billing API is an **allocation approximation**, not an assertion of a utility meter-read factor. Partial-cycle results must be treated as study estimates. SCE demand charges remain one complete monthly-cycle charge; a partial study does not prove the unseen peak.
- Local tax rate is separately confirmed or explicitly assumed. The implementation applies it to the recurring tariff subtotal; special jurisdiction-specific tax bases/exemptions are not modeled. State energy surcharge is $0.00030/kWh. A bill-confirmed SCE residential climate-credit amount can be applied once after local tax and state surcharge. No automatic award, cross-cycle credit ledger, or jurisdiction-specific franchise-fee treatment is modeled. Arrears, account corrections, special riders, optional RTEM/meter fees and other one-off items are excluded.

The optimizer and post-bill evaluator share `charge_lines`. Battery efficiencies, power/SOC bounds and final-energy equality use the existing `Battery` definition. Simultaneous charging/discharging and objective/bill disagreement are rejected. No AC network feasibility claim is made for schema 6.

## PV and solar settlement — material remaining scope

Confirmed **non-export** interconnection remains available, with surplus PV curtailed. Four bounded export paths are executable: **SCE bundled residential TOU-D-PRIME NBT23–NBT26**, **SCE bundled residential TOU-D-PRIME legacy NEM 1.0 or NEM-ST 2.0**, **LADWP residential R-1A NEM**, and **BWP residential EV TOU net billing**. SCE studies cover one confirmed cycle from January 1, 2025 through September 17, 2026; LADWP R-1A covers a net-import cycle in the same date range; BWP covers one complete cycle within July 1–September 20, 2026. An older NBT or NEM installation date selects its program rules, not historical 2021–2024 *service-period* prices. The browser and desktop GUI (which launches the same local browser workflow) expose these options. Commercial parallel-generation/standby eligibility must be confirmed separately. Comparisons exclude capital cost, incentives and lifetime payback. See the [SCE solar version audit](utility_sources/socal/solar_versions.md) for exact SCE account and date boundaries.

SCE NBT uses the [official Pacific-time EEC workbook](https://www.sce.com/customer-service-center/help-center/solar/solar-billing-plan/understanding-export-pricing) (SHA-256 `c88229eccf67e78f5e86c9ad4e1a8de7f946e585f49b39139621e70b55e211c7`), with its 1–24 workbook hours checked against the official MIDAS HS0–HS23 file. Separate delivery and generation export prices follow the confirmed NBT23/24/25/26 vintage, local service year, month, hour, weekday/weekend and SCE holiday. The 2025 and 2026 TOU-D-PRIME dated import sheets supply matching charges and NBC components. The [filed NBT rider](https://www.sce.com/sites/default/files/custom-files/PDF_Files/ELECTRIC_SCHEDULES_NBT.pdf) supplies credit restrictions and the non-equity ACC Plus adders: $0.040, $0.032, $0.024 and $0.016/kWh for NBT23 through NBT26 respectively. Delivery and generation EECs offset only their respective eligible energy charges; excess carries in separate banks. ACC Plus offsets remaining tariff charges, including NBCs and the base charge. A continuing cycle requires confirmed opening balances and a next relevant-period end after the cycle; the adapter does not calculate annual settlement. Taxes and state surcharge remain outside those credits. Local utility tax follows the existing study assumption on pre-credit import charges; actual jurisdiction-specific solar tax treatment needs account reconciliation.

NBT dispatch uses PV for load first. The battery may charge only from surplus PV and discharge only to residual load; it cannot grid-charge or export. This avoids claiming SCE's paired-storage export credits without implementing its generation-meter and monthly cap rules. Storage dispatch minimizes cycle cost **before inherited credits**, optionally including battery wear. The bill evaluator applies confirmed opening credits afterward and reports the closing balance. Newly earned export credits remain in the dispatch objective when usable this cycle; inherited credits do not change the schedule. **It does not assign future value to unused export credits**, so the result is not a full-year operating optimum or necessarily the lowest current cash payment. The credit ledger appears in saved results and CSV exports. Annual true-up, the Energy Export Credit Adjustment, Net Surplus Compensation, bonus equity classes, later import-rate versions and CCA generation remain outside this bounded path.

Legacy SCE NEM uses retail TOU energy netting, not the NBT workbook. A confirmed NEM 1.0 account needs an interconnection request and PTO by June 30, 2017; NEM-ST 2.0 needs a request before April 15, 2023, a PTO from July 1, 2017 onward, and a still-valid 20-year legacy period. Only bundled TOU-D-PRIME with monthly billing and no paired storage is modeled. NEM-ST 2.0 protects PPPC, NDC, CTC and WFC on each 15-minute net import; NEM 1.0 does not use that successor charge rule. The cycle must stay within one filed import-rate version and season. A confirmed opening dollar energy-credit balance can offset eligible energy, with unused credit reported in the ledger. Basic or base service charges, NBCs, and the study's tax/surcharge remain payable. Annual true-up, net-surplus compensation, annual billing, aggregation, CCA generation and jurisdiction-specific solar tax treatment remain outside scope. The same account-qualified calculation runs in the browser and desktop GUI; see [filed NEM](https://www.sce.com/sites/default/files/custom-files/PDF_Files/ELECTRIC_SCHEDULES_NEM.pdf) and [NEM-ST](https://www.sce.com/sites/default/files/custom-files/PDF_Files/ELECTRIC_SCHEDULES_NEM-ST.pdf).

LADWP's [filed residential NEM rider](https://www.ladwp.com/account/customer-service/electric-rates/residential-rates) and [NEM guidelines](https://www.ladwp.com/sites/default/files/documents/LADWP_Amended_Net_Energy_Metering_Guidelines_Document_January_2021_.pdf) direct billing-period kWh netting when the meter is a net importer. The R-1A adapter nets the two 15-minute meter channels over the confirmed cycle before applying the existing tier, minimum and dated adjustment rules. An account-confirmed opening dollar credit may offset only charges above the R-1A minimum and eligible adjustments; minimum charges and the study's local-tax amount are protected. The amount used and closing balance are exported in the credit ledger. Credits do not transfer automatically from another saved study: the next cycle must enter the previous closing balance as a new confirmed opening balance. At R-1A, with nonnegative net imports and equal initial/final battery energy, battery cycling cannot improve the bill because losses increase net kWh; the dispatch therefore leaves storage idle. **Net-export cycles reject** because the public rider does not state which R-1A tier prices excess kWh. R-1B TOU NEM credit allocation, account termination, multi-meter transfer, and exact jurisdiction-specific solar tax treatment remain unsupported.

BWP's [new net-billing rules](https://www.burbankwaterandpower.com/documents/d/guest/12-4-2025-2025-rules-and-regulations), [adopted ACOE rates](https://www.burbankwaterandpower.com/documents/d/guest/burbank_adopted_fee_schedule), and [sample solar bill](https://www.burbankwaterandpower.com/solar-net-bill-breakdown) support one residential EV TOU cycle within July 1–September 20, 2026. The customer must confirm EV enrollment, interconnected AC nameplate, a qualifying permit/upgrade/new-customer transfer on or after January 1, 2026, service size, the $0.034/kWh ECAC, the 7% UUT and an opening dollar credit (or first cycle). Fifteen-minute exports earn on/mid/off avoided-cost credits. Earned plus opening credit offsets imported energy and ECAC up to zero; the unused balance carries forward. The $19.50 customer charge and size charge remain payable. Both 7% charges apply to the post-credit usage subtotal plus fixed charges; the $0.0003/kWh state surcharge applies to imported kWh. The independent sample's displayed rounded components reconcile to $30.17; model results retain unrounded study amounts. The PV-first battery can charge only from surplus PV, discharge only to residual load, and never export stored/grid energy. Dispatch prices newly earned usable credits but ignores inherited credit when scheduling. Annual optional cash-out, legacy NEM, other BWP schedules, special riders and automatic credit linkage between saved studies remain unsupported.

PV uses the repository's existing WeatherDerivedPV chain (PVWatts, Hay–Davies, SAPM temperature, PVWatts inverter) with explicit clear-sky assumptions: 20 °C air, 1 m/s wind, 14% system losses, 96% inverter efficiency, DC/AC ratio 1.2. Diagnostics and weather export with the bill. Historical weather and equipment selection remain available in existing Bay Area paths but are not yet wired to schema 6. This limitation must not be described as measured production or a weather forecast.

The official solar documents were researched and archived; full settlement is **unfinished**:

- LADWP NEM outside the bounded R-1A net-import cycle: verified net-export credit-tier pricing, R-1B TOU credit allocation, and account-termination treatment still need implementation and bill reconciliation.
- SCE NBT and NEM beyond the bounded cycles: pre-2025 service-period import rates, other SCE billing plans, equity ACC Plus qualifications, paired-storage export caps, annual Energy Export Credit Adjustment, NEM/NBT true-up and Net Surplus Compensation.
- BWP beyond the bounded EV TOU cycle: Basic and commercial solar billing, grandfathered NEM, annual cash-out and dated rates outside July–September 20, 2026.

The full requested solar-program/vintage/export/true-up scope is therefore not complete. Enabling export programs requires additional implementation and tests, not simply enabling the form options.

## Architecture and saved studies

Schema 6 uses the existing HTTP server, durable SQLite queue, independent worker, immutable Python engine snapshot, saved requests, itemized result tables and CSV downloads. The form consumes the candidate's plan metadata; server-side eligibility is authoritative. Account/location changes invalidate confirmation; date and plan changes invalidate schedule acceptance. Old schemas and saved results remain readable.

The ordinary `/api/studies` route rejects schema 6. Submit through `/api/v1/socal/studies` with a saved `resolution_id`; injected client map evidence is rejected. The worker revalidates against its immutable engine. Each run stores engine identity, dependency versions, tariff-data version and adjustment versions.

## Residential refinement — September 19, 2026

SCE residential uses the shared generic `RatePlan` timeline with utility-specific immutable rate versions. Numeric billing and convex dispatch use the same dated charge expressions. Applied versions, source sheets, service dates and baseline quantities are saved in the result table and CSV. Runtime source data are Python modules so immutable workers retain their exact rates.

The November 15, 2025 boundary switches the pre-BSC basic/minimum rules to the daily Base Services Charge. Daily charges count local service days, including DST. Apartment/unit profiles select the multifamily basic charge. D minimum comparisons exclude WFC; generation, FRC and state surcharge remain outside that minimum. HPWH allowance is limited to TOU-D 4–9/5–8.

**Remaining allocation limitation:** baseline quantities and minimum charges across rate or seasonal changes use day-prorated subperiods. Preliminary H includes consumption-based seasonal allocation; exact utility meter-read reconciliation across these boundaries is not implemented. The result explicitly warns about this approximation. A negative bill exposes amount due and remaining credit separately, without claiming a multi-bill carry-forward ledger.

See [historical sources](utility_sources/socal/history_2025/README.md) and [validation](validation/SCE_Residential_Refinement.md). CCA, additional municipal providers and export settlement remain deferred.
