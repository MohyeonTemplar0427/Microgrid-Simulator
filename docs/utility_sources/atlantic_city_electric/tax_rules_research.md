# Atlantic City Electric New Jersey: tax and billing rules

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: ordinary tax-paying residential RS and single-phase MGS-Secondary customers receiving utility BGS-RSCP, with utility-approved parallel PV/storage only when the approval retains the modeled tariff. This is rule research, not approval of a production adapter or a claim that every 2025–2026 month has all rates. It deliberately separates utility/rate eligibility, tax classification, interconnection approval, and wholesale PJM metadata.

## Tax treatment

RS and MGS explicitly state that their published charges contain Corporate Business Tax (CBT) and New Jersey Sales and Use Tax (SUT). Ordinary RS and MGS must use the tax-inclusive published rate columns; adding a separate CBT or sales-tax multiplier would double count tax. BGS/rider tables must likewise use their explicitly inclusive columns.

Rider SUT says the factor changed to **1.06625 effective January 1, 2018**. The earlier 1.06875 printed in the same historical explanation is superseded. Thus the ordinary 6.625% SUT is embedded, including for residential customers. This differs from Maryland's residential exemption. The February/October 2025 sheets and current October 2026 sheets repeat the rule; the current rider's December 1, 2025 footer does not establish a new tax percentage.

Rider CBT explains a historical provision applied to base rates before January 1, 1998 and prints `1.3518% [1 plus the "a" factor...]`. That internally awkward historical notation is not a new adder or a reliable formula for reconstructing modern rates. Use the filed inclusive charges directly.

Exemptions require separate account facts. The riders list franchised utilities, certain pre-March-10-1997 cogenerators, pre-1998 special-contract customers, federal agencies and international organizations, plus specified qualified manufacturing/enterprise-zone businesses after the Company receives a Treasury sales-tax exemption letter. Do not infer an exemption from coordinates, town, business description, PV, storage or ordinary tax-exempt nonprofit status. The initial scope excludes all exemptions, special contracts, house-of-worship/veterans special provisions and one-time/late-payment/connection fees. No additional county/local energy-tax adder is identified by these ordinary published RS/MGS schedules. This is tariff treatment, not a statewide legal opinion that no local tax can ever exist.

## Eligibility, ordinary charges, minimum and quantities

RS serves individually metered domestic residences. Section II §4.5 excludes three-phase service and motors over5hp from residential service; §4.6 distinguishes regularly commercial parts of a residence. Initial scope must be utility-assigned ordinary RS rather than inferring class from the load shape. MGS-Secondary serves the entire requirements at one point, metered or compensated to delivery voltage, and excludes residential customers. Initial MGS is utility-confirmed single-phase secondary service, ordinary BGS-RSCP, demand-metered, without reactive-demand billing, special worship/veterans provisions, fixed-load contract, grandfathered heating exclusion or standby charges.

RS has a $6.75 monthly customer charge in the February/October 2025 and current October 2026 base sheets. The first distribution block is **750kWh in June–September**, 500kWh in October–May; current winter first/excess rates are equal. Do not confuse the separate BGS energy-block rules with distribution tiers. The two RS sheets do **not** state the MGS nearest0.01kWh rule. No general Section II rounding rule was found in the targeted review, so applying MGS quantity rounding to RS would be an unsupported extension. No separate RS `max(customer_charge,total_bill)` minimum is established by those sheets.

MGS base sheet has demand charges on **all billing kW**, not only kW above5. Transmission demand is separately charged for billing kW **above3**. The minimum monthly bill equals the applicable customer charge plus applicable adjustment. The October 2025 single-phase customer is12.55; October 2026 is13.99. This is not an additional minimum charge to add to the ordinary customer charge.

MGS demand is the greatest15-minute use during the month from the Company's demand meter. **Demand used for billing rounds to the nearest0.1kW**; **energy used for billing rounds to the nearest0.01kWh**. The precise tie convention at0.05kW or0.005kWh is unstated; reject exact half ties rather than inventing half-up/banker's rounding. No ratchet is introduced by the ordinary MGS demand sheet. Where there is no demand meter the sheet only provides estimated demand for **June1–September30**, computed as monthlykWh/150; it does not establish a winter formula. Unmetered methods are outside the initial scope.

Because ordinary MGS distribution demand is charged from the first billedkW, there is no useful positive-demand branch where every demand charge disappears. Optimizing rawkWh/raw15-minute peak can be a disclosed proxy objective, but its bound/optimality proof must not be represented as proof of the rounded final cash bill. Rounded numeric bills and raw-quantity objective bills require separate reconstruction/audits. This note does not approve a particular optimizer implementation.

## Billing-period and transition restriction

Section II §6.5 says monthly bills for periods other than **30days** must be prorated. It does not provide the precise component-by-component fixed-charge, demand, tier or midcycle rate-allocation formula. Do not import another utility's25–35day tolerance or assume days/30 applies identically to every ACE component.

Initial executable scope can therefore require **exactly30 complete local civil days**, wholly inside one verified rate regime and one summer/winter season. Reject31/28/29day calendars, partial days, missing intervals, effective-date crossings and season crossings until corresponding allocation evidence exists. A concrete potential cycle is October2–November1 exclusive, meaning all30dates October2–31, provided every applicable component is verified unchanged across those dates. The span must be defined in the account timezone, not as an unconditional720UTC-hour assertion; DST and actual interval coverage still require validation. This supports bounded cycles, not a full-year2025 claim.

Historical continuity is now independently preserved in the November21,2025 BPU order. ExhibitD redline physical249 (page40/157), printed SectionII sheet17, shows the prior leaf effective **December1,2023** and unchanged §6.5 monthly/other-than30day-proration text. Its clean counterpart is physical96 (ExhibitC39/151). Physical244 (D35/157), printed sheet13, shows the prior leaf effective **July5,2024** and unchanged §4.7 prior-written-permission text; its clean counterpart is physical92 (C35/151). Rendered review verifies these clauses are unchanged black text, rather than added text whose formatting was lost during extraction. The actual redline edits concern §4.5 detached buildings and administrative/footer details. This establishes ordinary billing-period and prior-parallel-permission continuity for2025; it still does not supply a component-by-component proration formula.

## Residential fixed deferral/recovery

October2025 SectionIV physical56/printed46, filed June30 effectiveJuly1,2025, explicitly applies to all residential customers July1,2025–February28,2026. RS receives a **$30 monthly credit July/August2025** and a **$10 monthly charge September2025–February2026**, both inclusive of SUT. It is mandatory ordinary relief/recovery, not an account-specific low-income program. Differences between credited/recovered totals are reconciled through CIP. Do not add another tax to these amounts.

The leaf does not state a specific carryforward/refund rule for a negative customer balance, whether a charge is conditioned on that same account receiving a prior credit, or an irregular-cycle posting formula. Do not invent an energy bank/export credit for the relief amount. If the bill may become negative, settlement outside the modeled ordinary bill requires separate support. A positive ordinary30day bill can show the published fixed credit as a charge component without claiming a cash refund. October2025 requires the +10 recovery even if all other charge tables are otherwise complete. For2026 beyond February, do not keep an exclusion or recovery amount solely because it existed in the prior months; verify current riders independently.

## PV/storage, NEM and historical standby

Section II §4.7 requires **prior written permission before installation**, and written permission for parallel operation. Rider NEM additionally requires application at least30days before service activation, prior approval before installation and final written approval before operation; applicable technical standards, protection, inspections and local approvals remain prerequisites. Location and simulated no-export behavior do not prove approval.

Both the2025 and2026 NEM riders cover eligible own-operated ClassI renewable resources, including solar, on/contiguous to the premises, connected in parallel and intended primarily to offset customer requirements. Mere storage ownership does not establish renewable NEM eligibility. Initial PV-plus-storage must have explicit utility approval for that combined configuration and the modeled non-export operating mode, including any grid charging. Standalone storage is not presumed NEM eligible. A study bound such as25kW is not a substitute for account-specific classification and approval.

The NEM rider retains ordinary schedule rates/components/billing units and says only perkWh components are affected. Customer and applicable measured demand charges remain. Under a zero-export constraint and zero opening NEM credit balance, the model can bill positive import using ordinary delivery/transmission/BGS rates without implementing export settlement. Do not simulate the rider's annual export bank, PJM residual-metered-load credit, avoided cost or cashout. RGGI/PJM metadata or wholesale prices do not substitute for tariff export settlement.

**Historical standby change matters:** February/October2025 STB leaves apply to MGS/AGS/TGS/SPP customers with other sources and standby service, and are unavailable when current-plus-prior-five-month GenerationAvailability does not exceed50%. GenerationAvailability is producedkWh divided by standbycapacity times4380hours. There is **no100kW threshold in these2025 leaves**. The current October2026 STB leaf instead requires standby when non-intermittent own generation available during an outage exceeds100kW nameplate. Do not apply the current100kW threshold retroactively. The September2026 book physical43/printed34 preserves the revised first STB leaf with an explicit **December1,2025** effective date. The November21,2025 BPU base-rate order additionally includes the new clean STB text at physical166, adopts the entire stipulation at physical8, and requires revised tariffs for service from December1,2025. These primary pins establish the changed threshold from that date; it is not inferred from the current continued leaf alone.

The initial implementation can therefore support MGS2025 grid-only while rejecting its DER cases, avoiding an unimplemented six-month generation-availability history. MGS2026 DER may use the verified post-December2025 threshold, a conservative combined PV/storage nameplate study bound of25kW, and explicit utility confirmation that ordinary MGS/noSTB classification is retained for the approved configuration and cycle. RS is outside the STB rider's listed classes. Interconnection/network upgrades, one-time charges, other generators, QF/SPP, special standby contracts and account-specific interconnection fees are outside the ordinary recurring-bill scope, not assumedzero for a real installation.

These limits must be carried into the source ledger and documentation; ordinary charges can only be called complete when delivery/supply/credit agents establish all components for the actual30day cycle and none of these unmodeled account conditions apply.
