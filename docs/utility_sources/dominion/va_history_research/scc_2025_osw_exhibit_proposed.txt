With respect to cost allocation, the Company proposes to use Factor 1 based on the average and 
excess methodology to allocate demand-related costs and benefits, and Factor 3 to allocate energy- 
related costs and benefits. Mr. Hewett also explains how the Virginia Clean Economy Act impacts 
the allocation of costs for Rider OSW, specifically with respect to customers that are statutorily 
exempt from non-bypassable charges.
Mr. Hewett also provides the projected bill impact of Rider OSW and the project, by year, over 
the life of the rider for a residential customer’s monthly bill, as well as examples of projected bill 
impacts for small general service customers and large general service customers, as directed by 
the Commission’s Final Order in Case No. PUR-2021-00142. Mr. Hewett also provides two long­
term bill analysis sensitivities as directed by the Commission’s Final Order in Case No. PUR- 
2022-00187. One analysis accounts for projected load growth by applying projected allocation 
factors and sales growth rates derived from the Company’s internal load forecast while the other 
holds constant current allocation factors and customer load over the life of the rider.
Mr. Hewett is sponsoring Filing Schedule 46C, which provides the details of the Company’s 
methodology for allocating the Rider OSW revenue requirement.
The implementation of the proposed Rider OSW, beginning on September 1, 2025, will increase 
the typical residential customer’s monthly bill, based on 1,000 kWh usage per month, by $2.60 
compared to the current Rider OSW.

10 Q. Would you please discuss the methodology for calculating the Rider OSW rates?
The Applicants have calculated the Rider OSW rates in accordance with the same11 A.
methodology used and approved by the Commission in the 2023 Rider OSW proceeding.12
13 In order to develop Rider OSW rates applicable to each of the Applicants’ rate schedules,
the Applicants must first determine the forecasted kilowatt-hour (“kWh”) sales for each14
of the rate schedules. For the Virginia jurisdiction, the Applicants forecast kWh sales and15
customers by “Revenue Class” (z.e., Residential, Commercial, and Industrial). This16
Revenue Class kWh sales forecast is shown on page 1 of Schedule 3. Accordingly, the17
18 Applicants’ forecasted kWh sales for September 1, 2025 through August 31, 2026 must
then be allocated at the rate schedule level. This allocation was performed using 2021 to19
2023 historical monthly customer and kWh usage for each rate schedule to capture the20
recent trends of kWh sales and the number of customers within each rate schedule. This21
allocation by Revenue Class (and within Revenue Class by rate schedule) is shown on22
