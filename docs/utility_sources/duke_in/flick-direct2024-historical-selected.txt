Q. WHAT IS THE BASIS FOR THE PROPOSED TOU CHANGES? 3 
A. Generally speaking, TOU rates can include a variety of pricing and design options, but 4 
generally all TOU rates seek to align price signals to the cost differences that exist across 5 
time (days, seasons, hours) for the electrical grid. Grid operations require that supply match 6 
demand at any given point in time; thus, supply resources are called upon based on the 7 
level of system demand, which can vary greatly across days and seasons. Increasingly, 8 
intermittent and non-dispatchable supply resources (e.g., wind and solar) are changing the 9 
supply/demand relationship, calling for changes in operational capabilities for the other 10 
supply resources, but also creating greater opportunities for price responsive demand. The 11 
Midcontinent Independent System Operator’s (“MISO”) choice to move to a seasonal 12 
capacity construct is evidence of the increasing complexity of supply/demand dynamics. 13 
Modern rate design seeks not only to recover the costs of providing service to customers 14 
based on their use of the system, but also to provide price signals so that customers who 15 
can respond to price signals can do so in a personally meaningful and informed manner. To 16 
maximize a TOU rate’s utility, pricing needs to be properly structured and aligned with on 17 
and off-peak periods thus ensuring proper signaling. The Company’s existing TOU periods 18 
increasingly do not align with current and anticipated system needs. Therefore, the 19 
Company is proposing to terminate the Company’s legacy TOU rates and will offer 20
