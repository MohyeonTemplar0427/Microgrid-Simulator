## Ordinary monthly cycles and rate boundaries

PartII §3.03 says charges are monthly and meters ordinarily read as nearly as practicable every30days. The tariff's month generally means consecutive regularly scheduled meter readings. §3.04 explicitly prorates monthly charges **only for billing periods outside26–35days inclusive**, using days-in-period/30. Both historical and current versions retain this text unchanged.

Therefore a complete ordinary calendar cycle of28/29/30/31local civil days can retain full monthly charges when every dated rate component is verified. Do **not** impose ACE's exact30day restriction. Importantly, **§3.07** (historicalphysical21/current28, printedII11) expressly makes charges apply to **service rendered on and after the specified effective date**, unless otherwise designated. This gives a source basis for applying a purely linear perkWh change to actual timestamped service energy on the correct side of its effective date, while retaining unchanged full-cycle customer charges and unchanged monthly600/1000kWh blocks. That allocation uses measured/modelled interval energy, not invented uniform daily consumption or whole-month bill-date pricing. A specific rider clock overrides the default and must be checked separately.

Delivery source comparison establishes that September15,2025 changes do not change RS/GS delivery customer/distribution/tier rates, and July15,2026 changes only the linear transmission price from.014877 to.017244; February15,2026 only removes the alreadyzero ZEC leaf. Supply/TEC must independently confirm all changed components are linear and no special clock applies before these crossings are enabled. For such verified linear crossings §3.07 supports interval allocation. Reject other crossings involving changed fixed, demand, tier prices/blocks, a season boundary, or an unsupported clock; no general formula for splitting those nonlinear components was established. A [FirstEnergy billing explanation](https://www.firstenergycorp.com/help/billingpayments/about_your_bill/bill_definitions/new_jersey_bill_definitions.html) confirms rate changes can produce a prorated bill but does not prescribe a conflicting days-weighted usage allocation. Full-year coverage still requires every component and crossing to be verified.

No general cash-cent rounding rule or RS/GS energy quantity rounding rule was established by the targeted PartsI/II review. Retain rawkWh unless another JCP&L-specific source establishes quantity rounding. Do not copy ACE's MGS0.01kWh rule. Missing intervals, timezone/DST transitions, duplicate timestamps and incomplete civil-day coverage require separate numeric validation. Proration by civil days must not be replaced by unconditional720UTC-hour logic.

PartII §3.02 requires separate billing of meters, except limited Company-operational exceptions. §3.15 permits computed unmetered service only for substantially invariable equipment with Company/customer agreement and written consent to operational/equipment changes. This is separate from GS's absence of a **demand** meter: ordinary GS may still meterkWh. The initial account has one electricity meter/point and excludes computed loads, private minimum/facilities agreements, unusual fluctuating loads (§3.16), special water heating, Day/Night, worship/veterans, traffic signals and other special provisions.

## GS demand, minimum and a useful small-account branch

PartIII GS physical13/printed12, original effectiveJune1,2024, defines billing demand as the greatest **15-minute integratedkW**, calculated to the nearest0.1kW. It does not specify the exact tie convention atx.x5kW. If metered demand is implemented later, preserve this rounding and its difference from a raw-peak objective; reject unverified exactties rather than invent half-up/banker's rounding. There is no corresponding ordinarykWh rounding clause on these leaves.

The same sheet explicitly says that **when the Company determines demand will not exceed10kW and elects not to install a demand meter, demand is considered less than10kW for billing**. This creates a supported utility-assigned small-GS branch without solving a demand-rounding graph. It is an account classification, not a classification inferred from the simulated peak or PLS. The study may conservatively require actual import15-minute peak strictlybelow10kW, retained no-demand-meter assignment, and independent confirmation that current/preceding11-month demand and contract demand do not exceed10kW. DER approval must retain this assignment rather than silently assuming the meter/classification would remain.

GS physical11/printed10 lists both ordinary current demand rates and the minimum-demand rate **in excess of10kW**. Physical12/printed11 makes the monthly distribution-demand charge the greater of the current-demand calculation and the minimum calculation using highest current-plus-preceding11-month demand, at least contract demand. Thus the minimum is a demand ratchet, not an extra fixed monthly charge. It does not disappear merely because the current modeled demand is low. Independent history/contract<=10kW confirmation makes both excess10 terms zero in the narrow branch; do not reset historical demand using PV/storage imports.

Current October2026 GS ordinary single-phase customer charge is4.65/month (three-phase16.69, excluded initially). Distribution energy uses fixed **1000kWh** first/excess blocks, not hours-use blocks: winter .069662/.006021; summer .075288/.006021. These literals are examples for the current leaf, not prices to extend backward. No separate whole-bill `max(customer_charge,total_bill)` minimum was found on ordinary RS/GS leaves; do not invent one. Private contract/facilities minima remain excluded account facts. General-service reconnect history also retains demand obligations across disconnection; initial continuous-service scope excludes that complexity.

## Tax already embedded

The ordinary RS/GS headings say **all published charges include SUT**. Customer and distribution charges explicitly include CBT. RiderCBT physical61/printed60 says CBT on non-production-related revenues is already included in rate schedules; it is not a separately added retail percentage. Do not assume CBT is an extra charge on generation production revenues.

RiderSUT physical62/printed61 says published charges incorporate the factor **1.06625**. Thus ordinary6.625% New Jersey SUT applies through filed tax-inclusive rates, including residential rates. Use the explicitly tax-inclusive rider columns/totals and do not multiply the assembled bill by1.06625 again. The January2025 and October2026 originals both preserve these June2024 leaves, independently establishing continuity.

Exemptions are separate account classifications: franchised utilities, certain pre-March10,1997 cogenerators, pre-1998 specially classified contracts, federal/international organizations, and specified qualified business/enterprise-zone cases. Qualified businesses require the Company to receive a Treasury exemption letter. Do not infer an exemption from coordinates, municipality, nonprofit status, PV or storage. No separate ordinary county energy-tax adder is identified in these RS/GS sheets; this does not prove that no special local/contract charge can ever exist. Initial scope is an explicitly ordinary taxable account with no exemption or special agreement. PartII §3.14 CIAC/advance/deposit tax grossups concern construction payments and are outside the recurring ordinary bill; do not silently treat installation costs aszero.

## Residential generation/capacity relief

RiderEGCC, physical66/printed65, filedJune27,2025/effectiveJuly1,2025, applies to **all full-service and delivery-service residential RS/RT/RGT customers**. It specifies **30monthly credit July/August2025** and **10monthly charge September2025–February2026**, inclusive of SUT. The current2026 book retains this dated historical leaf; its presence does not continue the fixed charge afterFebruary2026.

The regulatory asset is tracked monthly without carrying charges; its March1,2026 balance rolls into RiderNGC recovery. This is Company regulatory accounting, not an individual customer's NEM credit bank. Do not add both a fabricated deferred account balance and publishedNGC rates. The leaf does not establish individual negative-balance cashout/carry, credit proration, or conditioning the10charge on whether the same customer received an earlier30credit.

For ordinary positive-bill studies, include the mandatory dated credit/charge and reject unsupported negative postcredit settlement. Check **both unrounded and final component-cent-rounded totals** if the study uses component monetary rounding; rounding alone can turn a nearzero raw bill negative. Do not impose extra grid imports to satisfy this guard. If a DER optimum or grid case falls outside the domain, reject it transparently. No export/NEM settlement should be inferred from this separate residential rider.

## PV/storage, NEM and standby scope

Historical/current PartII §9.02 requires application; §9.04 permits connection only after formal Company approval; §9.05 requires applicable code/inspection evidence. Customer equipment/protection, special metering, testing, insurance and other interconnection costs depend on the approved application and are not modeled as free construction. §§9.06–9.17 address protection, disconnection, access and metering. These requirements do not by themselves establish a recurring standby exemption.

Historical/current §12.01–12.02 cover residential/commercial customers generating **ClassI renewable electricity behind their meter**, and require the stated sizing/annual usage qualifications. §12.05–12.06 require approved application/agreement and all applicable charges/standards. NEM initiation is the first regular meter-reading date at least20days after election/executed application, never before necessary meters are installed; application submission alone is not active-service approval. Initial DER must have already-effective approved ordinary service for the whole cycle. PV-plus-storage, grid charging and non-export controls require explicit configuration approval; standalone battery is not presumed renewable NEM eligible.

§12.07 sets monthly export-credit carry and annual avoided-wholesale-cost compensation, but initial implementation must enable **none** of that settlement: zero exports and zero opening credits only. Eligible own solar can serve onsite load and remaining imports can be billed at approved ordinary rates. Remote/aggregated/community solar, third-party credits, annual cashout, QFS generation sales and opening banks remain excluded. Wholesale PJM prices are not a substitute for retail export rules.

**JCP&L STB differs materially from ACE.** Historical January2025 and current October2026 PartIII physical59–60/printed58–59 retain originalJune1,2024 RiderSTB, applicable GS/GST/GP/GT. It is unavailable when GenerationAvailability (GA) for the current month does not exceed50%. AG uses current-plus-preceding11-month average on-peak generation divided by(260−scheduled-maintenancehours); GA=AG/ContractDemand. The rider modifies demand/minimum rules and adds a standby demand calculation. There is **no100kW exemption in these JCP&L leaves**, and a study25kW equipment bound does not establish exemption. RS is outside the STB listed classes.

A narrow GS DER account therefore needs affirmative utility evidence of approved ordinaryNEM/noSTB treatment **and retention of the no-demand-meter branch**; no inference from simulated solar capacity factor or demand. If that classification cannot be established for the hypothetical/real account, reject GS DER rather than implementing an unsourced small-generator exemption or silently omitting STB. A separate broad research pass into standby reform is unnecessary for the ordinary confirmed-account scope. The specific independent search for a current primary blanket NEM/noSTB rule did not establish one: an official StateLibrary code PDF found was an old2013 compilation, and archived2013 proposed standby material was not treated as a2025/26 adopted exemption. These are unresolved scope references, not rates used by the model.

This is a source feasibility statement, not evidence that annual simulation, bill reconciliation, electrical checks or browser acceptance have passed.
