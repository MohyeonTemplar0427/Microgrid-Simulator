# BGE Maryland tax and Universal Service research

Reviewed October 25, 2026. Research scope: ordinary R residential and G Type I
default electric supply, complete calendar cycles, non-export operation.

## Selected locality

Use an explicitly confirmed **unincorporated Anne Arundel County** account,
outside Annapolis and Highland Beach. This is a selected account fact, never
inferred from weather coordinates. No other localities are established here.

- [County Code section 4-6-104](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/annearundel/latest/annearundelco_md/0-0-0-152054),
  current 2026 S-92, subsection (c)(2): residential electricity is exempt.
- Subsection (c)(5): ordinary non-manufacturing electric deliveries are
  **$0.0025/kWh**. Manufacturing meters on P/GLP have a different rate and
  potential annual rebates; they are outside the selected G scope.
- This provision's amendment history ends with Bill 27-02. The utility's
  dated CSEG ledger independently prints the same ordinary Anne Arundel rate
  for every month in 2025 and January–October 2026. No July 2025 or July 2026
  change to this selected rate is present.
- [Maryland DLS Guide to Local Government Taxing Authority](https://dls.maryland.gov/pubs/prod/InterGovMatters/LocFinTaxRte/FinalGuidetoLocalGovernmentTaxingAuthority.pdf),
  August 2026, printed page 3, confirms the residential exemption and
  commercial/industrial scope.

## State sales tax and taxable base

[BGE Rider 3, P.S.C. Md. E-6 Suppl. 629, page 80](https://azure-na-assets.contentstack.com/v3/assets/blt71bfe6e8a1c2d265/blt3c6680987cbdeb5f/Rdr_3.pdf),
filed May 7, 2019 for June 2019 billings, states the ordinary state sales tax
is 6%, with residential electricity and specified exempt uses excluded.
Current [COMAR 03.06.01.10](https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/03.06.01.10)
provides the operative distinctions:

- D(1): electricity delivered under a residential/domestic PSC-filed schedule
  is exempt. R therefore has no ordinary state sales tax.
- C(2): county electricity taxes are excluded from state taxable price.
- C(3): the electric environmental surcharge is excluded from state taxable
  price.
- Production activity, government, nonprofit and diplomatic exemptions are
  separate facts; an ordinary small office does not acquire those exemptions.
- [COMAR 03.06.01.08](https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/03.06.01.08)
  A and B(1)(d) include ordinary consideration and vendor-imposed taxes in
  taxable price; D(5) excludes excise taxes imposed directly on the buyer.
  The broad base and explicit county/environmental exclusions are also in
  [Tax-General 11-101(l)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtg&section=11-101).

Ordinary G interpretation: apply 6% to utility supply, delivery and ordinary
rider consideration, including the passed-through franchise charge and USC;
exclude the separately stated county tax and environmental surcharge. This
follows the general taxable-price rule and its listed exclusions; no actual
ordinary G customer statement was recovered to independently calibrate that
tax-base interpretation. Do not describe it as customer-bill calibration.

## Universal Service Charge

Rider 3 above fixes R at **$0.32/month**. G uses the utility-assigned annual
delivery-service bill component bracket, not total supply plus delivery:

| Annual delivery component | Monthly USC |
| --- | ---: |
| Below $175 | $0.25 |
| $175–1,299 | $1.85 |
| $1,300–2,599 | $6.14 |
| $2,600–6,499 | $12.28 |
| $6,500–12,999 | $24.56 |
| $13,000–25,999 | $36.85 |

The original contains higher brackets too. A narrow small-office study can
require a separately confirmed $175–1,299 assignment and $1.85 monthly USC.
Retain that inherited assignment across grid-only, PV and storage scenarios.
Do not estimate it from one optimized month or dynamically replace account
history with hypothetical future annual bills. Reclassification/update timing
was not recovered from this sheet.

## Environmental and franchise components

- The original current BGE CSEG component ledger, supplied by the delivery
  research, prints **EES $0.000150/kWh** and **franchise $0.000620/kWh** for
  R and G in every 2025 month and January–October 2026.
  [Official original ledger](https://www.bge.com/cdn/assets/v3/assets/blt71bfe6e8a1c2d265/blt8ad3da6ba8d050f5/6acfc043f4251ab7b699fb93/Rdr32_CS_Current_Rates_12.pdf?branch=prod_alias).
- [Public Utilities 7-203](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/laws/StatuteText?article=gpu&section=7-203)
  authorizes EES and caps each account at the lesser of $0.000150/kWh or
  $1,000/month. The selected small-study domain is far below the dollar cap.
- [COMAR 20.50.01.04](https://regs.maryland.gov/us/md/exec/comar/20.50.01.04)
  specifies half-up cents for the separately itemized environmental charge.
- [2025 Senate Bill 316 official fiscal note](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2025RS/fnotes/bil_0006/sb0316.pdf),
  page 16, independently confirms the electric distribution franchise tax
  rate of $0.00062/kWh. This is separate from percentage gross-receipts tax
  already embedded in applicable filed utility prices.

## PSC assessment and double-counting

Rider 3 describes an assessment on regulated revenue but does not establish
another standalone customer percentage to add to all filed components.
Supply research recovered explicit SOS table columns: generation includes
its applicable tax column and transmission includes GRT and PSC assessment.
The delivery CSEG ledger's published distribution identity is exactly base
plus Riders 25/16/10/34, with no additional assessment column. Use the filed
published components without adding another PSC or wholesale tax factor.
This document does not independently prove every underlying revenue gross-up.
