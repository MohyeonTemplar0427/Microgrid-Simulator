EVIDENCE AND CONCLUSIONS FOR FINDING OF FACT NO. 2
N.C.G.S. §  62-133.2(c) sets out the verified, annualized information that each

EVIDENCE AND CONCLUSIONS FOR FINDING OF FACT NO. 3
The evidence supporting this finding of fact is contained in the direct testimony and
exhibits of DENC witnesses Hinson and Clemens.
Commission Rule R8-52(b) requires each electric utility to file a Fuel Procurement
Practices Report at least once every 10 years and each time the utility’s fuel procurement
practices change. DENC’s current fuel procurement practices for purposes of this
proceeding were filed with the Commission in Docket No. E -100, Sub 47A, on
November 30, 2023.
DENC witness Hinson’s direct testimony discussed the trends that affected fuel
commodity markets during the test period. Hinson Direct Test . at 2-8. Witness Hinson
stated that price volatility was and will likely continue to be prevalent across the natural gas,
coal, oil, and biomass fuel commodities. Id. at 2. For natural gas, key market fundamentals
continued to point toward weak commodity pricing witnessed at the end of the 2022–2023
season. Id. The initial natural gas price increase was largely caused by concerns, both
domestically and in Europe, about sufficient fuel storage inventories to meet anticipated
winter 2022–2023 demand. Id. at 2–3. The subsequent price decline occurred due to lower
natural gas prices resulting from overall mild weather, lower regional consumption, easing

7
international Liquid Natural Gas  (LNG) demand (and associated price decreases),
continued strength in domestic natural gas production, and a healthy domestic natural gas
storage inventory at the start of the 2023 injection season. Id.
Witness Hinson described DENC’s fuel procurement practices and explained that
DENC continues to follow the same procurement practices it has followed in the past in
accordance with its report filed in Docket No. E -100, Sub 47A. Id. at 4. He testified that
DENC continues to follow its fuel hedging program as discussed in its Fuel Procurement
Strategy Report, and that DENC believes its comprehensive approach to hedging ( e.g.,
price hedging, diverse fuel supply access, and diverse generation portfolio) has and
continues to have a material mitigating effect on fuel cost volatility . Id. at 4 –5. He also
discussed the activities that DENC deploys to mitigate fuel cost expenses in addition to
the hedging program while providing safe and reliable electricity for customers. Id.
Witness Hinson also explained the importance of both short -term and long -term
pipeline capacity acquisitions to provide greater access to competitively priced natural
gas supplies and greater fueling flexibility, due to the location of DENC’s gas-fired
generation fleet in the Mid -Atlantic region, which is characterized by frequent pipeline
constraints and high natural gas price volatility. Id. at 5–8. Witness Hinson discussed the
long-term pipeline and supply options DENC is considering, including executing
agreements that significantly improve fueling capabilities and continuing to pursue
incremental opportunities for firm pipelin e transportation, natural gas peaking services ,
and on-site LNG and/or oil fueling capabilities. Id. at 6–7.
Witness Hinson testified that DENC was recently awarded new pipeline service
encompassing natural gas liquefaction, 2.1 Bcf of storage capacity, and firm
transportation on the Cove Point LNG, LP pipeline.  Id. at 7. Additionally, DENC applied
to the Virginia State Corporation Commission (VSCC) in June 2024 for a CPCN to
construct and place in service a 2.0 Bcf LNG tank and associated liquefaction and
vaporization capabilities at the Greensville County Power Station location, serving both
Brunswick County and the Greensville County Power Stations. Id. Witness Hinson added
that the contracted Cove Point LNG service and LNG tank project will allow DENC to
replace third-party peaking supply, historically contracted at prevailing market prices, with
flexible fueling and delivery characteristics that align well with gen eration plan fueling
requirements using off-peak priced natural gas supplies. Id. at 7 –8. He explained that
both developments improve the reliability of DENC’s entire pipeline capacity portfolio,
help mitigate gas fuel costs by providing a competitively priced, firm natural gas supply
option during periods of high cost and high demand natural gas markets, and provide
incremental fuel assurance, across DENC’s natural gas fired generation fleet. Id.
Regarding natural gas procurement, witness Hinson explained that DENC
maintains a disciplined natural gas procurement plan to ensure a reliable supply of natural
gas at competitive prices.  Id. at 8.  He stated that t hrough periodic solicitations and the
open market DENC serves its natural gas-fired fleet using a combination of day -ahead,
monthly, seasonal, and multi -year physical gas supply purchases.  Id. Witness Hinson
also described how DENC evaluates its diverse portfolio of pipeline and storage contracts

Witness Clemens reported that the February 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine
and the resulting ongoing  conflict has continued to  impact the front -end nuclear fuel
component markets, with the impacts on conversion and enrichment markets being the
most pronounced. Id. at 2. He stated that both spot and term prices for conversion and
enrichment are significantly higher and are likely to remain higher than prior to the
invasion, as Russian supply becomes limited or unavailable. Id. at 2–3 He explained that

stated that the recently enacted U.S. government ban on Russian imports of enriched

front-end component contracts has reduced its exposure to the market price volatility that
has occurred over the past several years. Id. at 5.  Witness Clemens pointed out that
DENC has been active in the market in recent years and executed more market -based
and fixed price contracts, allowing it to take advantage of prior lower prices to benefit
customers. Id. He also noted that the 18-month refueling schedule for DENC’s nuclear
plants delays the full effect of any significant changes in a component price. Id. at 5–6.

EVIDENCE AND CONCLUSIONS FOR FINDING OF FACT NO. 6
The evidence supporting this finding of fact is contained in the direct testimony of

For purposes of determining the EMF rider, Commission Rule R8 -55(k) requires
that a utility must achieve either (a)  an actual system-wide nuclear capacity factor in the
test year that is at least equal to the national average capacity factor for nuclear
production facilities based on the most recent five-year period available as reflected in
the most recent Generating Availability Report of the North American Electric Reliability
Corporation (NERC), appropriately weighted for size and type of plant, or (b) an average
system-wide nuclear capacity factor, based upon a two -year simple average of the
system-wide capacity factors actually experienced in the test year and the preceding year,
that is at least equal to the national average capacity factor for nuclear production facilities
based on the most recent five-year period available as reflected in the most recent NERC
Generating Availability Report, appropriately weighted for size and type of plant.
Commission Rule R8-55(k) also provides that, if a utility does not meet either standard, a
rebuttable presumption is created that the increased cost of fuel was incurred
imprudently, and a disallowance may b e appropriate. Commission Rule  R8-55(d)(1)
provides that capacity factors for nuclear production facilities will be normalized based
generally on the national average for nuclear production facilities as reflected in the most
recent NERC Generating Availability Report, adjusted to refl ect the unique, inherent
characteristics of the utility facilities and any unusual events.

Public Staff witness Lawrence testified regarding DENC’s calculation of the billing
period rate. Lawrence Test. at 7. He explained that in this proceeding DENC changed the
methodology used to calculate the fuel cost for the billing period. Id. He continued that
previously DENC used the actual fuel expense from the most recent test period, applied
weather and customer growth adjustments, and adjust ed for known and measurable
changes to the generation fleet. Id. at 7–8. The result of this methodology was a reliance
on test period commodity costs, which in recent years varied significantly from the
projected billing period costs contributed to large EMF balances (both under - and over-
collections). Id. Under the new methodology used for this proceeding, the first part of the
process for determining the appropriate billing period fuel cost does not change – DENC
runs the production cost model and relies on the test period fuel expense, weather and
customer growth adjustments, and test period commodity costs. Id. Next, as part of the
new process, DENC re-runs the production cost model using projected fuel commodity
costs, instead of solely relying on test period commodity costs. Id.
Witness Lawrence also testified that this new methodology  results in system fuel
expenses that are $121.5 million higher than under the previous methodology. Id. He
testified that DENC subsequently applied the difference of $121.5 million as a line -item
adjustment to the total fuel cost.  Id. Witness Lawrence noted that the new methodology
should, in theory,  prevent or at least mitigate a future under -recovery compared to
DENC’s previous methodology. Id. He also noted that even with this line -adjustment
additur, fuel rates to recover billing period costs (the base fuel rate plus Rider A) will
decrease relative to rates which are currently in effect in this proceeding. Id. He concluded
that DENC’s change to the billing period fuel cost methodology addresses the Public
Staff’s concerns regarding this issue. Id.
