BY THE COMMISSION: North Carolina General Statute § 62-133.2 (Fuel Statute)
requires the Commission to conduct annual fuel charge adjustment proceedings for electric
utilities engaged in the generation or production of electricity by fossil or nuclear fuel s.
Commission Rule R8 -55 schedules an annual fuel and fuel -related costs adjustment
hearing for Virginia Electric and Power Company, d/b/a Dominion Energy North Carolina
(DENC) and requires that DENC use a test period of 12 months ending June 30.
On July 29, 2025, the North Carolina General Assembly enacted Senate Bill  266
as Session Law 2025-78 (S.L. 2025-78). Section 3 of S.L. 2025-78, entitled “Fuel Cost
Recovery Modifications,” amends the Fuel Statute  governing the framework for electric
public utilities to recover their fuel and fuel-related costs through annual rider adjustment

--- PDF page 2 ---
2

proceedings. Through S.L. 2025-78, the General Assembly amended the Fuel Statute by,
among other things: (1)  expanding the scope  of fuel and fuel-related costs that can be
recovered through annual fuel rider proceedings to include the total delivered cost of
power purchases and emission expenses; and (2)  amending the procedure for seeking
recovery of such costs, including requiring adjustments to avoid any potential double
recovery between fuel  and base rates. See N.C.G.S. § 62-133.2(a1), (a4), and (d).
S.L. 2025-78 did not introduce any new fuel or fuel-related costs that were not previously
recoverable but instead shifted the recovery mechanism for certain portions of these costs
from base rates to the fuel rider and established a new mechanism in subsection (a4) to
ensure that there is no double recovery of costs.
Commission Rule R8-55 provides that: (1) the fuel charge adjustment proceeding
for DENC will be held the third Tuesday of November each year; (2)  DENC shall file its
direct testimony; and (3) DENC shall publish notice prior to the hearing.

EVIDENCE AND CONCLUSION FOR FINDING OF FACT NO. 2
The evidence supporting this finding of fact is contained in the direct testimony,
exhibits, and workpapers of DENC witnesses Hinson and Brookmire.
Commission Rule R8-52(b) requires each electric utility to file a Fuel Procurement
Practices Report at least once every 10 years and each time the utility’s fuel procurement
practices change. DENC’s current fuel procurement practices  for purposes of this
proceeding were filed with the Commission in Docket No. E -100, Sub 47A, on
November 30, 2023.
In DENC witness Hinson’s direct testimony, he discussed the trends that affected
fuel commodity markets during the test period. He stated that price volatility was, and will
likely continue to be , prevalent across the natural gas, coal, oil, and biomass fuel
commodities. Hinson Direct Test. at 2.  DENC witness Hinson stated that  key market
fundamentals pointed to relatively strong pricing for natural gas when comparing the test
period to the prior year . He further noted that the summer of 2024 began with relatively
weak prices and a healthy domestic storage surplus alongside a production pullback .
However, witness Hinson indicated that production did not keep pace with strong power
generation demand and steady liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports, so the national
storage surplus diminished.  He stated that p ost-winter 2024/25, both U.S. and E .U.
storage inventories were lower than the prior year, supporting LNG export demand and
higher domestic gas prices.  Id. at 2–3. Witness Hinson testified that, although domestic
production had recently increased to near-record levels of about 106 billion cubic feet
(Bcf)/day, steady LNG export demand (about 15 Bcf/day currently,  with export capacity
additions that could reach up to 25 Bcf/day by 2028) and storage inventory shortfalls
contributed to an approximate 25% increase in natural gas prices for the test period
compared to the prior year. Id. at 3.
DENC w itness Hinson described DENC’s fuel procurement practices and
explained that DENC continued to follow the same procurement practices that it had in the
past in accordance with its report filed in Docket No. E-100, Sub 47A. He testified that
DENC continued to follow its fuel hedging program as discussed in its Fuel Procurement
Strategy Report and that DENC’s comprehensive approach to hedging ( e.g., price
hedging, diverse fuel supply access, and diverse generation portfolio) had, and continues
to have, a material mitigating effect on fuel cost volatility. Id. at 5. He also discussed the
activities that DENC deploys to mitigate fuel cost expenses while providing safe and
reliable electricity for customers. Id. at 5–6.

EVIDENCE AND CONCLUSIONS FOR FINDINGS OF FACT NOS. 4–5
The evidence supporting these findings of fact is contained in  DENC’s verified

For purposes of determining the EMF rider, Commission Rule R8-55(k) requires
that a utility must achieve either: (1) an actual system-wide nuclear capacity factor in
the test year that is at least equal to the national average capacity factor for nuclear
production facilities based on the most recent five-year period available as reflected in the
most recent Generating Availability Report of the North American Electric Reliability
Corporation (NERC), appropriately weighted for size and type of plant; or (2) an average
system-wide nuclear capacity factor, based upon  a two-year simple average of the
system-wide capacity factors actually experienced in the test year and the preceding
year, that is at least equal to the national  average capacity factor for nuclear production
facilities based on the most recent five-year period available as reflected in the most recent
NERC Generating Availability Report, appropriately weighted for size and type of plant.
Commission Rule  R8-55(k) provides that, if a utility does not meet either
standard, a rebuttable presumption is created that the increased cost of fuel was
incurred imprudently, and a disallowance may be appropriate. Commission Rule
R8-55(d)(1) provides that capacity factors for nuclear production facilities will be
normalized based generally on the national average for nuclear production facilities as
reflected in the most recent NERC Generating Availability Report, adjusted to reflect the
unique, inherent characteristics of the utility facilities and any unusual events.

EVIDENCE AND CONCLUSION FOR FINDING OF FACT NO. 9
The evidence supporting this  finding of fact is contained in the supplemental
testimony of DENC witnesses Farmer and DeJarnette and the direct testimony of Public
Staff witness Hill.
As previously noted above, on July 29, 2025, the North Carolina General Assembly
enacted S.L. 2025 -78. Although S.L. 2025.78 did not introduce any new fuel or
fuel-related costs that were not previously recoverable, t he new law amended, among
other things,  subsection (a1) of the Fuel Statute  to include the costs of purchases of
capacity by the electric public utility in the “cost of fuel and fuel -related costs” that are
recoverable through the Fuel Statute. Amended subsection (a3) of the Fuel Statute, the
subsection that applies to DENC, further provides that the customer class components for
purchased energy and capacity costs as permitted for recovery in subsection (4) of (a1)
must be determined on a class demand basis.
In DENC witness Farmer’s supplemental testimony, she updated DENC’s system
fuel expense for the test period to include the impact of S.L. 2025 -78 on the proposed
system fuel and fuel -related cost factors applicable to the February 1,  2026 through
January 31, 2027 rate year. She further  testified that DENC is a member of PJM and
participates in the PJM capacity market auctions to ensure that its capacity obligations
are met. DENC witness Farmer explained that DENC’s purchased capacity expense was
based on the cost of capacity resulting from those capacity auctions. She stated that a
certain level of these purchased capacity costs was recovered in DENC’s base rates as
approved in Docket No. E-22, Sub 694. DENC witness Farmer continued that, as enacted
on July 29, 2025, S.L. 2025 -78 amended the North Carolina Fuel Statute to provide for
recovery of purchased capacity related costs through the annual fuel factor proceeding.
As a result, she stated that DENC is now permitted to recover purchased capacity
expense through the annual fuel factor that is incremental to such expense already being
recovered through base rates. Farmer Supp. Test. at 2–3.
DENC witness Farmer further testified that as it relates to DENC, the revised Fuel
Statute at subsection (a3) provides that the purchased energy and capacity costs must
now be allocated among customer classes based on the electric public utility’s
North Carolina peak demand for the prior year. As a result, she presented the total system
fuel expense separated into two portions, with the first representing DENC owned
generation fuel expense and the second deriving from the purchased energy and capacity
fuel expense. DENC witness Farmer explained that the capacity costs included in the total
system fuel expense are based on the forecasted purchased capacity expense  for the
Rate Year based on the results of the PJM capacity auctions; this expense is reduced to

16

remove the portion already being recovered in base rates to prevent double recovery of
such expenses. Farmer Supp. Test. at 3–4.
In the supplemental testimony of DENC witness DeJarnette, he presented DENC’s
actual system fuel expenses and  North Carolina recovery experience for the fourteen
months that ended August 31, 2025. He explained that in addition to updating the actual
system fuel and purchased power energy expenses that DENC traditionally included in its
annual fuel factor proceedings, he also included for the July 29, 2025 through

purchased capacity expense already reflected in DENC’s base rates as approved in
Docket No. E-22, Sub 694. DeJarnette Supp. Test. at 1–3.
In Public Staff witness Hill’s direct testimony, he testified that he did not take issue

capacity costs for these months  are eligible for recovery in DENC’s next fuel rider
proceeding. Hill Supp. Test. at 9–10.
In DENC’s Letter in Lieu of Rebuttal Testimony, DENC did not object to Public Staff

Based upon the competent, material, and substantial evidence in the record , the

amended.
